SAFETY GUIDE

Everyday Safety Tips

Simple habits can reduce your risk and help you stay alert. No tip can guarantee safety, so trust your instincts and move to a safer place when something feels wrong.

👥 Walk in groups when possible

When travelling on foot, especially at night or in isolated areas, stay with people you trust rather than walking alone.

🚗 Never get into a stranger's car

Use transport you trust and verify the vehicle and driver before getting in. Never accept a lift from an unknown person simply because they offer one.

📱 Don't walk with your phone out

Keep your phone secure when walking. Avoid being distracted by texting, scrolling or wearing headphones in places where you need to stay alert.

👀 Stay aware of your surroundings

Look around you and pay attention to unusual behaviour, people following you or changes in your surroundings. Trust your instincts.

🌙 Avoid isolated areas at night

Choose busy, well-lit routes and avoid shortcuts through dark or deserted areas where possible.

📍 Tell someone where you are going

Let a trusted person know where you're going, especially when travelling alone or meeting someone you don't know well.

🚪 Check before opening the door

Don't automatically open your home or workplace door to strangers. Verify who they are before allowing access.

💳 Protect your personal information

Don't share passwords, PINs, banking details, one-time passwords or sensitive personal information with strangers.

🆘 If you think you're being followed

Don't go straight home. Move toward a busy public place, contact someone you trust and seek help. If you are in immediate danger, use the appropriate emergency service.

🏠 Make your home harder to target

Lock doors and windows, don't leave spare keys in obvious places, and avoid publicly advertising when your home will be empty.

🚨 If something feels wrong

Take that feeling seriously. Leave the situation if you safely can, get around other people and contact someone you trust.

🥋 Learn basic self-defence

Consider reputable self-defence training that focuses on awareness, avoidance, escape and getting to safety rather than looking for confrontation.

These are general safety reminders, not professional emergency advice. In an immediate emergency, prioritise getting to safety and contact the appropriate emergency service.
